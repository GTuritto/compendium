# Interpersonal Risk Taking

Interpersonal risk taking refers to behaviors in which team members make themselves vulnerable in front of others by exposing gaps in knowledge, admitting errors, or revealing uncertainty. This concept is central to understanding team dynamics and psychological safety in organizational settings.

## Definition and Characteristics

Interpersonal risk taking encompasses actions that carry potential social or professional consequences within a team context. Key behaviors include admitting mistakes and asking questions, both of which require individuals to acknowledge limitations or gaps in their understanding before colleagues.

## Relationship to Psychological Safety

Interpersonal risk taking is directly linked to psychological safety, which is defined as a shared belief that a team is safe for such risk taking. In psychologically safe environments, team members engage in interpersonal risk taking without fear of negative consequences such as embarrassment, rejection, or punishment. The presence or absence of psychological safety determines whether individuals feel comfortable engaging in these vulnerable behaviors.

## Abbreviations and rationale

- **Phase 2 ingestion scenarios were not re-walked.** The corpus was already
  ingested via the v0.1 hermetic walk; re-ingesting under real models would
  spend embeddings calls without changing the ingestion contract (ingestion
  does not call the embeddings seam; that happens in `index sync`/`reindex`,
  which was exercised in step A).
- **Phase 8 TUI scenarios were skipped.** The TUI is interactive and is
  validated by the manual smoke walk independently. Phase 1 does not change
  the TUI surface.
- **Phase 5.3 fallback scenario was not re-run.** The fallback path is
  exercised in the hermetic suite (`tests/test_retrieval.py` and the
  golden Category C); Phase 1 does not change retrieval behavior.

The combination of the live tests (which exercise the seams directly) and
the focused pipeline walk (which exercises both seams in service of the
real retrieval and synthesis paths) provides equivalent evidence that
"real models work end-to-end on the primary host", which is the Phase 1
goal stated in [`docs/COMPENDIUM_V0.2_BUILD.md`](../../docs/COMPENDIUM_V0.2_BUILD.md)
§ Phase 1.
